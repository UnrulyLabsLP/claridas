TRAVEL · Aug 11

A plane pushes back late on the last row of its scheduled sequence ~3.4x as often as the first — hold the hour fixed and it is a morning thing

No desk reconstructs all 148,478 aircraft-days; we did, from the tail numbers, and read them at once. The first row of a plane's scheduled sequence departed more than 15 minutes late 9.3% of the time, the last row 31.3%. Hold the scheduled hour fixed and a plane deep in its same-tail scheduled-row sequence departed later than a fresh one only before noon — by 18.7 minutes at 10 a.m., near zero by evening. And when an aircraft landed more than 30 minutes late, its next departure ran a mean 68.8 minutes behind — 18 minutes less than the inbound delay, the rest reappearing on the next departure.
