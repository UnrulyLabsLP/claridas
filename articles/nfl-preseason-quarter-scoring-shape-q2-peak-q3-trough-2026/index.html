We read all 33 NFL preseason games so far: scoring peaks in the second quarter and craters in the third

The fourth quarter, played almost entirely by backups, outscored the starters' opening quarter by 78 points. The scoring doesn't follow the talent off the field.

Filed by the Claridas sports pod · August 26, 2026

The Facts
We added up every quarter of every NFL preseason game played so far this August — all 33 of them, from the Aug. 7 Hall of Fame Game through the round that ended Aug. 24, the two exhibition weekends that come before the final dress-rehearsal round. Together they scored 1,200 points. Sorted by quarter, the scoring is lopsided: 244 in the first, 434 in the second, 200 in the third, 322 in the fourth. The second quarter alone holds 36% of all the points and outscores the third by better than two to one — 2.17x. The first half outscored the second, 678 to 522, but the shape inside is not a slide. It is a peak, a crater, and a rebound. The fourth quarter — played almost entirely by backups and roster-bubble players — outscored the first quarter, the starters' quarter, by 78 points, 322 to 244. And the pattern is not one loud weekend: read the two exhibition rounds separately, and in each the second quarter is the highest- scoring and the third the lowest. Of the 33 games, 16 were within one score entering the fourth quarter and 13 finished within one. The Analysis
The following is analysis, not fact. The broadcast intuition about a preseason game is a slide: the starters open, the talent bleeds off the field drive by drive, and by the fourth quarter you are watching players who will be cut on Tuesday. Scoring, the intuition says, should fall as the roster empties. The whole-record read says it doesn't. Scoring climbs to its high right before halftime, collapses to less than half of that in the third quarter, then climbs back up in the fourth — the quarter with the least NFL talent on the field outscoring the quarter with the most. The number that carries the story is the third quarter: 200 points, the fewest of any frame, in the stretch right after the break when you would expect fresh legs, not the fewest points of the night. A plausible read of the shape — offered as hypothesis, not proof — is that the second-quarter spike is a two-minute-drill artifact, offenses pushing to score before the half while the best available players are still in, and that the fourth-quarter rebound is roster-bubble urgency: players auditioning for jobs, in a hurry, against a defense doing the same sspeculative. The third quarter is the dead zone in between — wholesale substitutions, feeling-out, no one yet desperate sspeculative. What the data pins down is the timing, not the cause: the points arrive on a curve the box score never plots, and it is not the curve the talent chart would draw.
Room for Disagreement
This is 33 games, and preseason is a small, noisy sample — a few garbage-time drives can move a single quarter's total. More important, the read stops one round short of the whole: the dress-rehearsal round, when starters traditionally stay in through the third quarter, is scheduled for Aug. 27–29 and is not in this count. If starters do play deeper in that round, the third-quarter crater could flatten — the pattern here belongs to the two early rounds, when most first units were done by halftime, and should not be sold as a law of the whole preseason. The second-quarter peak may also owe as much to clock mechanics as to who is on the field.
