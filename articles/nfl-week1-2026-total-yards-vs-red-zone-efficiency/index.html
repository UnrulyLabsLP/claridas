The Facts

Houston came out of Sunday afternoon with 4 red zone touchdowns on 5 trips — an 80 percent conversion rate. Buffalo finished with 1 on 3 trips, 33 percent. The Bills won, 36–31. Across all 16 games ESPN listed as final for Week 1 of the 2026 NFL regular season, the team with the higher red zone touchdown conversion rate won 6, lost 7, and matched its opponent 3 times. Total offensive yards fared better: the team that gained more yards won 14 of 16. Third-down conversion rate landed between them — 12 of 16 went to the team that moved the chains more often. Buffalo scored 3 touchdowns outside the red zone. Josh Allen scored on a 21-yard scramble. DJ Moore caught a 43-yard throw. Joshua Palmer caught a 34-yard score. None of those plays logged a red zone attempt; in the metric every broadcast cited, they did not register. Houston's 4 touchdowns all came from inside the 20. By red zone conversion rate Houston came out ahead; on the scoreboard, Buffalo did. Two other games ran the same direction. Washington converted all 3 of its red zone trips to touchdowns — a perfect rate — and lost to Philadelphia 24–22. The Eagles' winning score was a 43-yard pass from Jalen Hurts to Dallas Goedert in the fourth quarter, a touchdown scored from outside the 20. Seattle beat New England 13–10 without converting either of its red zone trips; its 13 points came from 2 field goals and a 45-yard touchdown reception by Jaxon Smith-Njigba. The reverse also appeared. Green Bay gained 180 more yards than Minnesota — 419 to 239 — and lost 39–22. The Vikings converted all 4 of their red zone trips to touchdowns (100 percent); Green Bay converted 1 of 4 (25 percent). Green Bay led 22–17 entering the fourth quarter, then Minnesota outscored the Packers 22–0 in the quarter — Green Bay's only scoreless quarter of the game. Minnesota converted 100 percent of its red zone trips against Green Bay's 25 percent; that gap may account for how total yards (419 to 239, Green Bay) diverged from the final score (39–22, Minnesota) mmodeled.

The Analysis

The following is analysis, not fact. Total yards counts offensive scoring plays from scrimmage — the 43-yard catch in the flat, the 81-yard post route, the 21-yard scramble — while red zone conversion rate counts only what happens inside the 20. A team that scores multiple touchdowns from long range accrues yardage without logging a single red zone attempt; its efficiency registers nowhere in the broadcast metric. In a week where several final scores appeared to hinge on explosive plays before the 20-yard line, a measure of short-field execution would miss most of that scoring. sspeculative The 2 exceptions to total yards point in different directions. Green Bay outgained Minnesota by 180 yards and lost; the 75-percentage-point red zone conversion gap (100 percent to 25 percent) is the figure most consistent with offsetting the yardage mmodeled. New Orleans outgained Detroit by 100 yards and lost in overtime; both teams finished at an identical 80 percent red zone rate, and New Orleans committed 3 turnovers to Detroit's 1. In the second case the red zone rates were identical, so the yardage exception appears to align with the turnover margin and overtime rather than with any red zone conversion gap mmodeled. The 2 exceptions are structurally different, which limits what can be inferred from them as a pair.

Room for Disagreement

Red zone efficiency is not the same as red zone volume. A team that reaches the red zone more often can score more red zone points even at a lower conversion rate, which is one reason the Houston situation looks unusual: the Texans' 5 trips to Buffalo's 3 is a wide gap, and Houston also committed 2 turnovers to Buffalo's none — a factor the red zone metric does not record. The same caveat applies to New England's 3 turnovers in its loss to Seattle; the red zone rate for each winner was worse, and turnovers may have limited scoring chances that the rate never counted mmodeled. Across all 3 games where the loser scored more red zone touchdowns than the winner — Houston–Buffalo, New England–Seattle, and Washington–Philadelphia — the winner committed no more turnovers than the loser in any of them; in 2 of the 3, the winner committed fewer (Buffalo 0 to Houston's 2; Seattle 0 to New England's 3). In the Washington–Philadelphia game, both teams committed zero turnovers. A full-season sample would need to disentangle this turnover pattern from the red zone story. And a 16-game sample does not establish a season-long pattern. A week in which explosive plays were frequent may look different from a week built on field-position grinding.
