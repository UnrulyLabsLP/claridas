We read all 32 kickers: accuracy barely moved the 2025 fantasy order — attempts did, and the offense owns those

One primary kicker per team, the full 2025 regular season. Field-goal accuracy ranged 66.7% to 96.6% and correlated 0.39 with fantasy points. Attempts correlated 0.95 — and attempts belong to the offense, not the leg.

Filed by the Claridas sports pod · August 22, 2026

The Facts
The kicker is the one fantasy roster slot people draft on visible individual skill: the leg, the long, the field-goal percentage. So we read every kicker in the 2025 regular season — one primary per team, the highest-volume kicker for each of the 32 clubs, from nflverse's complete season aggregate — and scored each on the standard distance-weighted system: three points for a made field goal inside 40 yards, four from 40–49, five from 50-plus, one per extra point, re-derived from the per-distance make counts. The skill that separates these 32 is real and wide. Field-goal accuracy ran from 66.7% (Joshua Karty, eight games) to 96.6% (Nick Folk and Eddy Pineiro), a spread of nearly 30 points. It barely touched the fantasy order. Across the 32, accuracy correlated 0.39 with fantasy points; field-goal attempts correlated 0.95. Restrict the pool to the 25 kickers who played 15 or more games — stripping out the ones who missed time — and accuracy's correlation falls to 0.26 while attempts holds at 0.90. Swap the distance-weighted scoring for a flat three points a field goal and the split is the same: accuracy 0.38, attempts 0.94. The individual cases carry it. Jason Myers of Seattle led every kicker with 202 fantasy points — on the most kicking chances in the league, 96 in all (48 field-goal attempts, 48 extra points) — while his 85.4% field-goal rate ranked 14th among those 25 full-season kickers. Nick Folk, tied for the most accurate at 96.6% (28 of 29), finished 21st. The eight primary kickers who converted 90% or more of their field goals finished 2nd, 4th, 6th, 13th, 18th, 21st, 26th and 32nd in fantasy points — the full width of the field. Riley Patterson (93.1%) and Chad Ryland (75.8%) sit 17.3 percentage points apart in accuracy and two fantasy points apart in the total: 132 to 130.
The Analysis
The following is analysis, not fact. A kicker's fantasy points are paid per attempt, and the attempts are not his to generate. Every extra point requires a touchdown; every field goal requires a drive that stalls inside range. Both are properties of the offense in front of the kicker, not of his leg mmodeled. Jake Bates converted 79.4% of his kicks, fourth-worst among the full-season group, and finished 8th in fantasy points — because Detroit's offense handed him 54 extra points, more than any kicker in the league got. Myers led the position on Seattle's volume, not his precision. So the number a manager can see when evaluating a kicker (accuracy, the long, the highlight from 58 yards) and the number that actually scores (attempts) sit on opposite sides of the ledger. The consensus draft board treats kicker as a skill position priced on the leg mmodeled. Read across the whole pool, the 2025 season priced it as an offense position wearing the kicker's jersey.
Room for Disagreement
Scoring systems vary, and the gap narrows under some of them. Leagues that dock points for missed field goals or add distance bonuses would reward accuracy more than the standard system here does — though attempts still dominated even under flat three-point scoring (0.94 to 0.38) mmodeled. Games played is itself part of "attempts," so a kicker who stays healthy for 17 games banks an edge unrelated to his offense; the 15-plus-game restriction narrows that but does not fully isolate team scoring from kicker durability mmodeled. And accuracy still bites at the margin the season totals can't show: a kicker who misses enough loses the job, a selection effect that quietly trims the bottom of the accuracy range before Week 1 sspeculative.
The View From
The manager on the clock in the last round looks at field-goal percentage and leg strength — the two things a kicker controls. The full-pool read says the number that pays is printed on someone else's scoreboard.
