We priced all 31,210 fantasy opportunities of 2025: in PPR a target is worth 2.64 carries, 1.60 in standard

A carry and a target are the two atoms of fantasy scoring, and they are not priced alike. Read the complete 2025 ledger and the gap holds even inside the running-back pool — the 55 backs with 100-plus opportunities ran on 81% of their chances but banked only 63% of their points there, and made the other 37% on the 19% that came through the air.

Filed by the Claridas sports pod · August 26, 2026

The Facts
A single-team manager counts touches as one number: my back got 20, my receiver got 8. Read the complete 2025 opportunity ledger and that number is two different currencies quietly added together. In nflverse's full 2025 regular-season file — every player, all 18 weeks — the NFL logged 14,601 rushing carries and 16,609 pass targets, 31,210 opportunities in all, and they do not pay the same. Priced in full-PPR scoring (0.1 per rushing or receiving yard, 6 per touchdown, 1.0 per catch), the average carry returned 0.645 fantasy points and the average target 1.705 — a target was worth 2.64 carries. The gap is set by the rulebook, not the field: strip the reception point and run the same 2025 season in standard scoring and a target falls to 1.60 carries; half-PPR puts it at 2.12. Restrict the count to the running backs, receivers and tight ends managers actually draft and the full-PPR ratio rises to 2.7. The tell is that the gap survives inside a single position, where preference can't explain it. The 55 running backs who cleared 100 opportunities in 2025 took 81.3% of their chances as carries — but those carries produced only 63.4% of their fantasy points, while the 18.7% that came as targets produced 36.6%. Christian McCaffrey finished the overall PPR RB1 on 311 carries and 129 targets; Jonathan Taylor carried a league-high 323 times at 0.958 points per opportunity, and James Cook — 309 carries against 40 targets — ran on 88.5% of his chances, among the most carry-dependent workloads in the pool, at 0.883 points per opportunity.
The Analysis
The following is analysis, not fact. The opportunity-versus-output spine of fantasy football usually gets read as a question of how much: how many carries, how many targets. The 2025 ledger says the prior question is what kind. A carry and a target are both usage — both volume a manager accumulates across a season mmodeled — and both show up in the same touch column — but one books a point the instant the ball is caught, before a yard is gained, and the other does not. At the league's 67.5% catch rate — a reception on roughly two of every three targets — that reception point alone lifts the average target most of the way to its 2.64-carry price. That is why the running-back split matters more than the cross-position one. It would be easy to read 1.705 versus 0.645 as receivers simply out-earning runners. But the same kind of gap — wider still — opens inside one back's own stat line: 29% of McCaffrey's opportunities came through the air, and they produced 57% of his fantasy points, a target worth 3.16 carries to him. The manager adding a carry and a catch into one touch total is adding two numbers denominated in different units, and weighting them equally understates the target pile.
Room for Disagreement
Per opportunity is not per season. Running backs still crowd the top of PPR boards precisely because they command the volume: McCaffrey saw 440 opportunities in 2025 to the most-used receiver's 188 (ranking receivers by total opportunities), and a rate disadvantage loses to that much raw usage. The ratio is also a scoring-rules artifact, not a law of football — it collapses toward 1.60 in standard leagues, so half of the point here is about how a commissioner set the scoring, and league settings vary. And a carry can carry value this per-opportunity frame doesn't capture: short-yardage and goal-line carries are widely modeled as worth more than the average one, carries drain the game clock, and rushing volume is conventionally steadier week to week than target volume, which depends on staying on the field for pass downs. Those are established characterizations of the position, not measurements in this season-total ledger, which captures neither leverage nor week-to-week variance mmodeled. This is the completed 2025 record, not a projection of 2026.
The View From
From inside one roster, a touch is a touch and 20 beats 8. From the whole 2025 ledger, the two atoms of that count are priced 2.64 to 1 in PPR — and the season's most carry-heavy backs banked their smallest slice of work at the highest rate.
