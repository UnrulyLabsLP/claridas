In 2024 the world's second-largest net gainer of people was Ukraine — two years after it recorded the biggest one-year population outflow of any country this decade

We summed the UN's net-migration estimate for all 217 countries. Ukraine swings from −5,699,445 in 2022 to +1,146,012 in 2024, behind only the United States — and read whole, 2024's ledger tracks where wars paused more than where opportunity concentrates.

Filed by the Claridas world pod · August 22, 2026

The Facts
In 2022, the first year of Russia's full-scale invasion, Ukraine recorded a net loss of 5,699,445 people — the largest single-year net outflow of any country in the United Nations' net-migration series this decade. Two years later the same series shows Ukraine gaining 1,146,012, the second-largest net inflow of any country on Earth in 2024, behind only the United States. mmodeled We pulled the UN Population Division's net-migration estimate — immigrants minus emigrants, the figure the World Bank publishes as indicator SM.POP.NETM — for all 217 countries and territories it covers, from the release dated 13 July 2026 (the 2024 revision). The United States led at +1,286,132. Behind Ukraine came Syria (+546,494), the United Kingdom (+417,114) and Canada (+368,599). The largest net departures were Pakistan (−1,401,173), India (−630,830), Sudan (−544,257) and Bangladesh (−473,362). mmodeled Of the 217, 88 countries gained people, 128 lost them, and one netted exactly zero. Summed, they net to +28,366 — about 0.4% of the 6.93 million people the receiving side gained — because the UN builds the series to balance globally: every emigrant is, in principle, someone else's immigrant. So the total is not the story. The composition is. Read one country at a time, 2024's ledger looks like the economics-textbook magnet list has broken; read whole, its biggest movements sit where conflict has recently started, stalled, or eased. mmodeled
The Analysis
The following is analysis, not fact. A net-migration figure is a residual, not a count of arrivals: it nets everyone who left against everyone who came. A country can therefore post a large positive number simply because a wartime exodus stopped while a fraction of those who fled trickle back — without out-attracting anyone. That is the most likely reading of the Ukraine reversal: the 2022 outflow ending plus partial returns, not a nation that suddenly out-pulled the United States. sspeculative The steadier caution is Syria. The UN series shows it a net receiver every year from 2020 through 2024 — before the fall of the Assad government in December 2024. Whatever drives Syria's estimated inflows, it predates the event most readers would reach for, which is a useful reminder that the residual moves for reasons the headline rarely names. mmodeled Across the full set, the year's largest swings in human movement track the phases of conflict — its onset (Sudan, −544,257), its pause (Ukraine) — more visibly than wages do. That is a pattern these figures are consistent with, not a mechanism a residual can isolate. sspeculative
Room for Disagreement
The strongest counter is that these are modeled estimates, not counted arrivals, and net migration for active-conflict countries is among the least certain figures the UN produces. UNHCR still counted about 5.2 million Ukrainian refugees abroad at the end of November 2024, and a March 2025 survey found only 43% intend to return, down from 74% two years earlier — so a positive net-migration line does not mean the displacement reversed. mmodeled The +1,146,012 is fully consistent with the outflow merely stopping, which a residual cannot distinguish from mass return. Read it as "Ukraine stopped hemorrhaging people," not "Ukraine refilled." A second caveat: the near-zero global sum is imposed by the method, not observed, so it proves nothing about data quality — it is the reason we read the ranking instead of the total.
The View From
**View from Kyiv:** every returning family is real, but the government's own concern is the millions still abroad and the falling share who plan to come home; a one-year net-positive estimate is not repopulation. mmodeled **View from the UN statisticians:** the series is designed to be globally consistent and is revised often; single-year, single-country figures for war zones carry wide error bands and should be read as direction, not decimals. mmodeled
