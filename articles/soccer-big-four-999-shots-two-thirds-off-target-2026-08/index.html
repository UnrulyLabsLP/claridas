We counted every shot in Europe's big four last weekend: 999 of them, two-thirds never hit the target

Across 38 matches in England, Spain, Italy and France on Aug. 21-24, teams put 327 of 999 shots on target and scored 98 — one goal for every 10 attempts. Barcelona scored 5 on 11 shots; Angers took 20, hit the target twice, and scored none.

Filed by the Claridas sports pod · August 25, 2026

The Facts
We counted every shot. Over four days, across the top divisions of England, Spain, Italy and France, players took 999 shots — and 672 of them never hit the target. The population is the complete slate of matches played Aug. 21-24 in the four leagues that were open: 10 in the Premier League, 10 in Serie A, 9 in La Liga and 9 in Ligue 1 — 38 matches and 76 team performances in all. Germany's Bundesliga had not yet opened its season and is excluded. The funnel is steep. Of the 999 shots, 327 were on target — 32.7% — and 98 became goals. That is one goal for every 10 shots taken (9.8%), and roughly one for every three shots that actually forced the goalkeeper to make a play (30.0%). The median team took 12 shots and put 4 on target. Nineteen of the 76 teams managed two on-target shots or fewer; 27 failed to score at all (team-scored-zero, not clean sheets), and four matches ended 0-0.
The Analysis
The box score treats a shot as a chance. The complete read says it usually isn't. Two-thirds of everything struck last weekend flew wide, high, or into a defender — the raw shot count measures ambition, not danger. Volume and finishing came apart cleanly. Barcelona took 11 shots at Elche, put 7 on target, and scored 5. Angers, the same weekend, took 20 shots against Lille, hit the target twice, and scored none. Liverpool led every team this weekend with 27 shots at Newcastle — 20 of them off target — and left with a 2-2 draw. More shots is not more goals; over a full weekend it is often just more misses. Which number tracked the scoreboard? Among the 28 matches that produced a winner, the team that put more shots on target won 20, lost 7, and matched its opponent's on-target count once. The team with more total shots won 19. The team with more of the ball won just 16 — barely better than a coin flip. Each comparison runs over the 28 matches with a winner; a match tied on that metric counts as neither win nor loss for it — on-target shooting had one such tie, total shots and possession none. Across this weekend, possession was the weakest of the three signals and on-target shooting the strongest, though the gap between the two shot measures is a single match. These are associations in one weekend's data, not causes mmodeled.
Room for Disagreement
The honest limits are sample and definition. This is one weekend — 38 matches — not a season, and conversion rates swing hard over short windows; a single clinical afternoon like Barcelona's 5-0 pulls the goals-per-shot figure up mmodeled. The gap between on-target shooting (20 of 28) and total shots (19 of 28) as a result-picker is one match — effectively a tie, not evidence that one is the better guide. And counting shots ignores where they were taken from: a six-yard tap-in and a 30-yard hopeful each count as one in this ledger. An expected-goals model, which weights chance quality, would refine every rate here mmodeled. What the complete read fixes is the scale — how much of elite-level shooting simply misses.
The View From
From a single match, Liverpool's 27 shots looked like a siege. Read across all 38, it looks ordinary: shots are cheap and the target is rare, and the teams that won mostly hit it more than they out-shot anyone. The eye remembers the barrage; the ledger remembers that 672 of the weekend's 999 attempts never made a goalkeeper move.
