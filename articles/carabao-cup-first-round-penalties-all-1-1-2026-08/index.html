The Facts
You followed one club through the first round of the English Football League Cup. We read all 34 ties at once, and from that seat one shape stands out: of every scoreline that finished level, only one appeared. From 7 to 10 August 2026 the first round of the Carabao Cup produced 34 completed ties among clubs from the Championship, League One and League Two, plus two Premier League entrants, Wolverhampton Wanderers and West Ham United; the rest of the Premier League joins in later rounds. The 34 ties yielded 84 goals, an average of 2.5 per match. Twenty-six were settled inside 90 minutes; the other eight finished level, and every one of the eight was level at 1-1 — not a single 0-0, not a single 2-2, not a 3-3 anywhere in the round. With no extra time played, all eight level ties went straight from the 90th minute to a penalty shootout. The shootouts split evenly by venue: the home side won four (AFC Wimbledon 5-4 over Newport County, Burnley 4-3 over Notts County, Barnsley 6-5 over Wigan Athletic, Preston North End 4-3 over Huddersfield Town) and the away side won four (Shrewsbury Town 4-3 at Salford City, Reading 5-4 at Bromley, Doncaster Rovers 5-4 at Stockport County, Millwall 2-0 at Queens Park Rangers). Across all 34 ties the home team advanced 19 times to the away team's 15 — a 55.9% home-advance rate. Queens Park Rangers is the round's sharpest reversal: it held 56.4% of the ball and out-shot Millwall 11 to 5, drew 1-1, then scored zero of its penalties and lost the shootout 2-0. Ball dominance did not guarantee a result: Burnley kept 73.8% of the ball against Notts County and still needed penalties, and Derby County kept 71.1% against Lincoln City but lost 1-2, out-shot 14 to 8 by a visitor that touched the ball less than a third of the match.
The Analysis
The following is analysis, not fact. Read one match report at a time and this is an ordinary opening week of cup football: some favorites advanced, a few were held, a handful of shootouts went the way shootouts go. We read all 34 ties at once, and from that seat two structural facts surface that no single scoreline shows. The first is a rule made visible by its results. The League Cup runs its early rounds with no extra time, sending any tie level after 90 minutes directly to penalties; from 7 to 10 August that rule decided 8 of 34 places in the second round — 23.5% of the round, close to one in four — on a shootout rather than on play. The second is the clustering, and it is the thing only the whole read surfaces: of every scoreline that could have ended level, only one did. Eight 1-1s, and nothing else — no goalless stalemate, no 2-2 — so the entire round's supply of drawn football arrived at exactly the same number. That is the kind of pattern a shootout erases the moment it starts: the record advances the winner and forgets that all eight got there identically. Underneath sits the quieter result. The first round is the one that drops higher-division clubs onto lower-division grounds, so the home ledger is worth reading on its own. Across the 34 ties the home side advanced 19 times to the away side's 15 — a 55.9% split — and in the eight shootouts, the tightest ties of all, home and away advanced four apiece. We do not carry a modeled baseline for what home advance "should" be in this specific format, so we report the split rather than grade it. Ball control tracked the result no more cleanly: Burnley (73.8%) and Derby County (71.1%) each dominated possession without winning in regulation, and Queens Park Rangers out-possessed and out-shot its opponent only to miss every penalty it took. None of it proves possession or shot count is worthless; in these ties the side with more of either did not reliably win in regulation.
Room for Disagreement
We read the whole round, but the whole round is still one round of one season — 34 ties, 8 shootouts — and every number here describes four days, not a law. A 55.9% home-advance rate across 34 knockouts is well inside the range chance alone would produce; flip three of the home results and home advantage looks no better than even, and nothing in this record distinguishes a real effect from noise at this N mmodeled. The eight-for-eight 1-1 clustering is striking but partly mechanical: with no extra time, any level tie becomes a shootout, and 1-1 is simply the most common way two evenly matched sides finish — the surprise is the absence of a single 0-0 or 2-2, not the presence of the 1-1s.
