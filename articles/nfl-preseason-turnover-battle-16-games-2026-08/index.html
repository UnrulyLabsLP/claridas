We read all 16 NFL preseason games at once: turnover margin picked the winner in just 7 — and 4 won it backward

The complete 13–16 August slate — all 32 teams, one game each, one 13-13 tie. The team with more first downs won 13 of the 15 decided games; the team that lost the turnover margin still won 4 of them. Indianapolis turned the ball over four times, out-gained New England anyway, and settled for a tie.

The Facts
We read every box score from this week's NFL preseason slate — all 16 games, so you don't have to scroll 16 game pages — and one column behaved nothing like its reputation. Between 13 and 16 August 2026, all 32 NFL teams played exactly once: 16 games, every one now final, with one tie (Indianapolis 13, New England 13). That makes a complete population, not a sample — every club, one game each, the whole round. Turnover margin is the number football reaches for first. Give the ball away fewer times than the other side, the thinking goes, and you have all but won mmodeled. Across the 15 decided games this week, the team that committed fewer turnovers won 7. In 4 games the turnover counts were even (Pittsburgh–Green Bay, Arizona–Las Vegas, Tennessee–San Francisco, Los Angeles Rams–Kansas City), and in 4 more the winner lost the turnover battle outright: the Los Angeles Chargers beat Houston 27-7 while giving it away twice to Houston's once; Jacksonville beat New Orleans 24-20 on 2 turnovers to 1; Baltimore beat Philadelphia 24-7 despite 2 giveaways to Philadelphia's 0; and Dallas beat Seattle 17-7 with the game's only turnover. Read the other way: of the 11 games in which one side gave the ball away more than the other, the team that protected it better won 7. Compare that with the plainer columns. The team with more first downs won 13 of the 15 decided games; the team that ran more offensive plays won 12; the team with more total yards, a higher yards-per-play, and a longer time of possession each won 11. Turnover margin — 7 — sat alone at the bottom. Every one of those turnover figures reconciles: we re-derived each team's total from fumbles lost plus interceptions thrown, and all 16 games balanced (Indianapolis's four, for instance, is 2 fumbles lost and 2 interceptions; New England's is zero of each). The whole slate produced 33 turnovers across 32 team-performances, roughly 2 a game. The tie is the cleanest illustration. Indianapolis out-gained New England 355 yards to 275, made 21 first downs to 16, and averaged 5.6 yards a play to 4.0 — and turned the ball over four times to New England's none. It was enough to tie, not win: Spencer Shrader kicked a 46-yard field goal early in the fourth quarter and a 61-yarder with 1:16 left to reach 13-13, and neither team scored again. The team that lost the ball four times controlled the game by each volume measure here — yards, first downs, yards per play; the team that never lost it could not win. The turnover column pointed one way and the scoreboard sat still.
