We read all 15 of MLS's Wednesday games: the team with more of the ball won 4 and lost 8

Across the complete August 19 slate, the side that kept more possession went 4-3-8 and the side that took more shots did worse (3-2-9). Only shots on target pointed the right way — and barely, 6-3-5.

Filed by the Claridas sports pod · August 21, 2026

The Facts
Major League Soccer played its entire midweek card on August 19, 2026 — 15 matches, all completed, 45 goals, an even 3.0 a game. We pulled the official box score for every one and read the three numbers a broadcast leads with: possession, total shots, and shots on target. Two of the three pointed at the losing team more often than the winner. The team that held more of the ball won 4, drew 3, and lost 8. Four of those losing possession leaders cleared 60%: San Diego kept 70.0% at Portland and lost 3-1; San Jose held 63.4% at LA and was shut out 1-0; Vancouver had 63.5% and lost 1-0 to Houston; Columbus had 61.8% and lost 2-1 to Montreal. Shot volume did worse. The team that took more shots won 3, drew 2, and lost 9, with one match level on shots. San Diego put up 21 shots to Portland's 11 and lost; San Jose took 22 to LA's 10 and lost; Vancouver managed 17 to Houston's 9 and lost. Only one of the three counts finished ahead of even money: shots on target. The team with more of them won 6, drew 3, and lost 5, with one match level on the metric. Vancouver's 17 shots produced a single shot on target. The road team, meanwhile, won 8 of the 15; the home team won 4, and 3 were drawn.
The Analysis
The following is analysis, not fact. On this slate the two figures fans reach for first — possession and shot count — were anti-signals: each described the team that lost more often than the team that won. That is not a claim that holding the ball hurts. It is that neither number measures the one thing that settles a match, which is whether the shots go in. Shots on target came closest to tracking the results, and even it only went 6-5 among the twelve decisive games. The cleanest illustration runs the other way: San Diego led Portland in possession, in total shots, and in shots on target, 6 to 5 — and still lost 3-1. When the best of the three proxies points at the loser, the box score is telling you the game was decided below the level any of these counts can see — chance quality and finishing. Vancouver's night, 17 shots and one on target for zero goals, is the same lesson in a single line. Read one match and a dominant possession side looks unlucky; read the whole card and eight of them lost on the same evening.
Room for Disagreement
This is one night, 15 games. Across a full season, possession and shot volume do correlate with winning — modestly, but really — so a Wednesday is a snapshot, not evidence that control is worthless sspeculative. Shots on target is the stronger proxy in the research on the question, yet here it went just 6-5 among decisive matches: no single count is a rule, and the actual driver is chance quality and conversion, which these box scores do not measure directly. The road side's 8-4 edge over the home side is the same kind of small-sample swing and should not be read as a trend. Every figure above is scoped to exactly the 15 matches played August 19, 2026, and nothing here forecasts any team's next result.
The View From
From inside a single match, a team with 70% of the ball and twice the shots looks unlucky to lose. Read all 15 at once and it stops looking like luck — eight possession leaders lost on the same night, and the thing being counted was never the thing that mattered.
